# How to use the Study Room

**For everybody in the app. Everything in here is yours.**

The Study Room is your own place to write while you read: an Explorer's notes on
a study, a Guide preparing one, a leader's notes on a sermon. It is not a chat,
it is not shared, and nobody is waiting for you to finish anything in it. Until
20 October 2026 it was for Explorers only, and marked Beta.

It is furnished like a study: the places in a bookcase, your pages on a desk
under a reading lamp, and the page you are writing on as a sheet of paper with
a margin and a ribbon to mark it. The furniture is drawn by the app itself, so
nothing extra is downloaded and it all works with no signal.

---

## Opening it, and leaving it

Tap **📖 Study Room** in My Rooms. It fills the whole screen, because it is a
room rather than a box on a page.

To leave, tap **← Hope Beacon** at the top left. That puts you back on your own
home screen in the app. Nothing is lost by leaving; there is no Save button
anywhere in this room and there does not need to be.

---

## Simple and Advanced

The room starts **Simple**: your pages, **+ New page**, search, and the **Bin**.
On a page you get its name, **Write** and **Whiteboard**, and the bar for adding
things: four basics, a heading, a list, a to-do and a quote, each with a
picture and a word, and **More** for everything else. That is the same in
Simple and Advanced, so nothing has to be found twice.

Tap **Advanced**, under the room's name, for the rest of the room: **Starred**
and the **Journal**, folders, tags and saved views, the star on each page,
**File this page**, and taking your pages to and from Obsidian. Tap **Simple**
to put them away again.

The choice is remembered on that phone or computer. Switching never hides or
changes anything you have written: a page you filed in a folder or tagged in
Advanced keeps its folder and tags, they are only out of sight in Simple. The
rest of this guide describes Advanced where it mentions those.

In **Focus** and **Dark Aero**, the looks with a dark page, the writing on a
page is light. Until 7 October 2026 it stayed almost black, on almost black.

## The shelf

The room opens on your pages rather than on a page, the same way the Library
opens on books.

**Each row is one page** and shows its name, the first line of what you have
written on it, and when you last wrote in it. Tap a row to open it.

**The places** are along the top on a phone and down the side on a bigger
screen. Simple shows the first and the last; Advanced shows all four:

| | |
|---|---|
| **All pages** | Everything you have, newest writing first |
| **Starred** | The ones you tap the star on (Advanced) |
| **Journal** | A page for each day (Advanced) |
| **Bin** | Pages you have put away |

**Search** looks at both the name and the words on the page, so you can find a
page by something you remember writing on it even if you never named it.

**+ New page** starts a fresh one and opens it straight away.

Your pages are grouped by when you last wrote in them: Today, Yesterday, Earlier
this week, This month, Older. That is usually how you are looking for them.

## Writing on a page

The first line is the **name of the page**. Type there to name it; the name is
what you will see on the shelf and in search. You can leave it blank and name it
later.

Below that is the page itself. Start typing.

### The bar: four basics, and More

Above the writing is a bar with four buttons, each a picture and a word:
**Heading**, **List**, **To-do** and **Quote**. Tap one and it appears on the
next line, with the cursor in it.

**More** opens everything else as a set of cards, each showing what it makes
before you choose it: **Big heading**, **Small heading**, **Numbered list**
("1. 2. 3. In order"), **Note** (a box to set something apart), **Line** (across
the page), **Table** ("Rows and columns"), **Today's date**, and **Picture**,
which opens the photos on your phone or the files on your computer.

This is how it works on a phone, where there is no `/` key. On a computer you can
also:

### Type `/` for anything that is not a plain sentence

Press the `/` key on a line and a menu opens. This is the one thing worth
remembering, because everything else lives behind it:

- **Heading 1, 2, 3** for the parts of a study
- **Bulleted list** and **Numbered list**
- **To-do** for things to come back to, with a box you can tick
- **Quote** for a verse or a line from a book
- **Divider** to separate one day from the next
- **Table** for a comparison
- **Callout** for something you want to stand out
- **Today**, **Tomorrow**, **Yesterday** to drop a date in
- **Image** and **Attachment** for a photograph or a file
- **Table view** and **Kanban** for a database you can sort and group
- **Equation** for mathematical notation
- **Linked doc** to point at another of your pages
- **Code block**, **Bookmark**, and the embeds

There are over four hundred entries in that menu, which is AFFiNE's whole list.
Start typing after the `/` to narrow it, then press Enter.

### Select words to format them

Highlight any words and a small bar appears with **bold**, *italic*, underline,
strikethrough, inline code, a link, and text colour. The same bar can turn a
paragraph into a heading or a list.

**On a phone**, the bar sits above the on-screen keyboard while you are typing.

### Keyboard shortcuts, if you are on a computer

| | |
|---|---|
| Bold | Ctrl+B (Cmd+B on a Mac) |
| Italic | Ctrl+I |
| Underline | Ctrl+U |
| Link | Ctrl+K |
| Undo | Ctrl+Z |

## Looking after your pages

**Star** a page with the star at the end of its row and it appears in Starred.
Tap again to unstar it.

**Move to bin** — the bin at the end of a page's row — takes a page off the
shelf without destroying it. It waits in the Bin until you decide.

**Put it back** returns a page from the Bin to All pages.

**Delete forever** lives only in the Bin, is the only red button in the room,
and asks you once before it does anything. After that the page is gone, from
this device and from the church's database, with any picture that only that
page used, and cannot be recovered. Until 20 October 2026 the page's writing
stayed behind in the database, and an emptied room could bring it back.

---

## Saving, and what to do if it says it has not saved

Your writing saves itself as you go. There is nothing to press. Under each
page's name a short line says where it is:

- **Saved, on this device and with your church.** Nothing to do.
- **Saving…** It is on its way; it usually takes a moment.
- **Kept on this device. It reaches your church when the connection is back.**
  You are writing without a signal. Keep writing: the room keeps everything on
  your phone or computer and sends it the next time there is a connection, even
  if you close the app first.

The room keeps a copy of your pages on the device you use, so it opens at once
and works with no signal. **Signing out deletes that copy**, so a shared
tablet keeps nothing of yours. (A private window in some browsers will not keep
a copy at all; there the room works straight from the database, as it always
did.)

If something is wrong, the room tells you instead of pretending:

**"This page has not arrived yet"** means you opened a page this device has not
fetched yet, usually on a slow connection the first time you use a new phone.
Only that page says so; the rest of the room keeps working. Tap **Try again**.

**"Your study room did not answer"** appears only on a device that has never
opened your room before, with no connection. Nothing has been lost; your pages
are in the database. Tap **Try again** when there is a signal.

**A red line at the top of the room** means one thing did not work (adding a
block, a copy for Obsidian). It goes away on its own or with **OK**, and the
room stays as it was. It used to replace the whole room until it was reopened.

## Who can see what you write

**Nobody.** Not your Guide, not a Director, not the person who set up the app.
That is true for everybody who has a study room, whatever their role.

If you are an Explorer, your Guide can see **that** you have been working in
your study room and **when** you last did. They cannot see the pages, the names
of the pages, or a single word you have written. That is deliberate and it is
enforced by the database rather than by a setting anybody can change.

## Tags, and finding things later

A page can carry tags: **Romans**, **Sabbath**, **Prayer meeting**, whatever you
would look for it by. Tap **File this page** under the page's name (Advanced),
then add them one at a time, pressing Enter or the comma key after each. The
button says how the page is filed, for example *In Romans study · 2 tags*, so
you can see it without opening it.

- The box **offers the tags you already use**, so you end up with one *Romans*
  rather than *Romans*, *romans* and *Romans 8*.
- On the shelf, every tag in your room is a row of buttons above the list. Tap
  one and the list narrows to it; tap **Every tag** to see them all again.
- The search box looks at tags as well as titles and words.

A page can have as many tags as you like, and the same page can sit under all of
them. That is the point of tags rather than folders: a page about Romans 8 is
about the book, the sermon it came from and the group it was written for, all at
once, and a folder would make you pick one.

---

## Folders and saved views

Three ways to find a page, and they are not the same thing.

**A tag** is what a page is *about*. A page can have as many as you like.

**A folder** is where you *put* it. One folder per page, and you never create
one: tap **File this page**, type a name under **Folder**, and the folder exists
because something is in it. When the last page leaves, it is gone.

- Folders appear as a row above the list. Tap one to see only what is in it.
- Start a new page while a folder is showing and it is filed there already.

**A saved view** is a *question* you keep. Narrow the shelf by a tag or a
search, press **Save this view**, and give it a name.

- It keeps the question, not the pages. A page you write next week that matches
  it turns up in it without you doing anything.
- Forgetting a saved view does not touch a single page.

If you are not sure which to use: tag everything, make a folder when a study
gets long enough that you want it in one place, and save a view when you find
yourself typing the same search twice.

## The journal: a page for each day

**Journal** (in the sidebar, or along the top on a phone) gives every day a
page of its own. Press **Today's page** and you are writing in today.

- It is named for the day, in full, so you never have to name it.
- Pressing Today again **opens the same page**, not a new one, however many
  times you press it and whatever you have renamed it to.
- The journal is ordered by the day each page is about, so correcting last
  Sabbath's notes does not move them above this morning's.

It is meant for morning devotion, a sermon, a prayer that was answered: the
things nobody names and everybody wants to find again.

## The whiteboard

Every page can be looked at two ways, and the switch above the page says
which: **Write** is the document, **Whiteboard** is the same page on an infinite
canvas. It is one page, not two: there is nothing to copy across. The
whiteboard is there in Simple too.

**The bar at the top of the board** has the tools, each a picture and a word:
**Select**, **Pen**, **Shape**, **Sticky note**, **Words**, **Arrow** and
**Eraser**, then **Undo**, **Redo** and **Show all** (fit everything into view).

**Tap something on the board** and the same bar changes to say what it is
("This shape:") and what you can do to it:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Write on it** | Put words in a shape, a sticky note or a line of words |
| **Colour** | Six colours: yellow, orange, red, green, blue, purple |
| **Copy** | A copy next to it, selected, ready to drag |
| **To front**, **To back** | Which one is on top when they overlap |
| **Delete** | Takes it off the board; **Undo** appears for a few seconds |
| **Done** | Back to the tools |

**Right-click** (or **press and hold** with a finger) on anything for the same
choices, or on an empty part of the board for **Undo**, **Redo**, **Select
everything** and **Show everything**.

The card with your page's writing is on the board too. It cannot be deleted
from here, so nothing written on the Write side is lost by tidying the board.

**Undo** works everywhere in the room: Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on a Mac) on a page or a
board, or **Undo** on the board's bar. Until 27 October 2026 it did nothing in
the Study Room; see `CHANGELOG.md`.

**In Advanced**, AFFiNE's full set of tools is also in a bar at the bottom of
the board: shape styles, pen colours, mind maps, frames and templates. Simple
shows only the room's own bar, so the same tools are never offered twice, once
with words and once without. Both are for the
things a column of text is bad at: how a passage is built, who is related to
whom, a plan for a study on a wall. On a phone or tablet a finger on the board
draws; it no longer scrolls the room behind it.

## Pictures, tables, databases and the rest

Everything AFFiNE can do, this room can do, because it is the same editor with
the same set of blocks. Press `/` on a computer, or use the bar of buttons on a
phone:

- **Pictures and files.** Put a photograph of a page straight into your notes.
  They are kept in the church's own database like everything else in your room,
  and they are still there tomorrow. A photo from a phone camera is made
  smaller by itself before it is kept, and **loses the record of where it was
  taken**, as every photo in the app does. Other files can be up to one
  megabyte, because the database is shared by the whole church; for a large
  document, keep it in My Files and put a link to it here.
- **Tables**, and **databases** with a **table view** and a **kanban board** if
  you want to track a study across weeks.
- **Equations**, in proper mathematical notation.
- **`@` to link to another page** of yours, or to a date. A page that links to
  another shows the link at the bottom of the page it points at, so you can walk
  back the way you came.
- **Bookmarks and embeds**: a link laid out as a card, a YouTube video, and a
  whole page of yours embedded inside another.
- **Code**, if you ever want it.

## The one thing that does not work properly

**Code has no colours.** The block works, keeps what you type and copies out
fine, but the words are all one colour. The thing that colours them needs a
permission this app's security rules do not grant, and widening that rule is a
security decision rather than a styling one. Say the word if you want it and it
will be put to you properly.

## If you get stuck

Anything the room cannot do, it says on the screen. If a message here does not
match what you are seeing, that is worth reporting: a screenshot of the room
with the message in it is the fastest way to get it fixed.
