# Code of conduct

This is a small project used by churches. The standard is ordinary decency, and
it is short enough to read in full.

## What is expected

- Assume the other person is trying to help. Most disagreements here are two
  people with different information, not one person being difficult.
- Criticise the code, not the person. "This breaks on a phone" is useful;
  "this is amateur work" is not.
- Remember who is reading. Issues and pull requests are public, and some of the
  people who open them are volunteers at a church rather than professional
  developers. A question that seems obvious to you is not obvious to them.
- Let people leave. Nobody here is on call, and an unanswered issue is not a
  slight.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discrimination, sexual attention or advances,
publishing anybody's private information, and sustained disruption of a
discussion after being asked to stop.

**Please also do not post real people's details** — names, addresses, contact
details, prayer requests, anything from a real church record — in an issue, a
pull request, or a screenshot. Use the sample church. A public repository is
indexed within minutes.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the repository owner through GitHub, at
[github.com/Klydo131](https://github.com/Klydo131). There is no dedicated
address and no committee; this is a small project maintained in spare time, and
saying so is better than implying a process that does not exist.

Reports are read. Anything credible is acted on: a warning, removal of content,
or a block, depending on what happened.

**A security vulnerability is not a conduct report.** Use the private
[Security Advisories](https://github.com/Klydo131/Open-Sentry-Beacon/security/advisories/new)
route instead — see [docs/SECURITY.md](docs/SECURITY.md).

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, and code comments.
